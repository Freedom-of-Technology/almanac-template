# Your records file: opening, saving and changing it

Your records files are kept on **GitHub**, a website that stores files safely and keeps every
earlier version. Your business has its own GitHub account. If GitHub asks you to sign in, use
that account.

Each domain has one records file. The list of your domains, with a link to each file, is on your
almanac's front page, under [Your records](../README.md#your-records). That's the link this guide
means by "the link for your domain".

## To read it

Click the link for your domain. The page shows the file. Each record is one line, and the lines
starting with `;` are notes saying what it's for.

## To save a copy on your computer

You need this to put everything back at your DNS company.

1. Click the link for your domain.
2. Just above the file, at the right, there's a row of small buttons. Click the one with an
   arrow pointing down into a tray. If you rest the mouse on it, it says **Download raw file**.
3. The file lands in your **Downloads** folder, with the domain's name, for example
   `freedomoftechnology.com.zone`.

Your computer may say it doesn't know how to open a `.zone` file. That's fine: you don't need
to open it, only to choose it when your DNS company asks for a file.

## To change it

1. Click the link for your domain.
2. In the same row of small buttons, click the **pencil**. If you rest the mouse on it, it says
   **Edit this file**.
3. Make your change. Copy the layout of the lines around it.
4. Click the green **Commit changes...** button at the top right.
5. In the box that opens, write a few words about what you changed, for example
   `Added Calendly's record`. Then click **Commit changes**.

If GitHub offers **Propose changes** instead, click it, then **Create pull request**. FreeOT
will see it and finish it for you.

## To see what changed, and who changed it

1. Click the link for your domain.
2. Just above the file, at the right, click **History** (it has a small clock).
3. You'll see a list of every change, newest first, each with who made it, when, and the few
   words they wrote about it.
4. Click one to see exactly what it changed. Lines in red were taken out; lines in green were
   put in.

## Made a mistake?

GitHub keeps every earlier version. Nothing you do here changes your website or email: only your
DNS company's settings do that. Ask for help and the file can be put back the way it was.
